Data Wagon sells two things to two audiences. Gamers buy virtual servers for Rust, Palworld or Garry's Mod and want to see their game before they see a spec. Businesses buy bare metal and resellers buy in bulk, and they want the spec sheet first and the artwork never.

The site handles that with one visual system and two tones. Every page shares the same dark header, red accent and typography, so moving between them never feels like switching sites. The gaming VPS page spends its hero on a honeycomb of game art. The dedicated servers page spends it on a world map and a promise of setup in fifteen minutes. Content sections sit on white below both.

The dedicated catalogue is the densest screen in the project. Servers are grouped by processor, with each CPU's configurations as rows beneath it, and the columns a bare metal buyer actually sorts by: bandwidth, storage, memory, setup time, price. Filters for location, hardware, price and availability sit at the top with a list or grid toggle, and a switch between enterprise and value lines keeps the two inventories apart. On the VPS side, the same instinct produced a plan comparison where the four cards stay short and a full spec table does the comparing underneath.

The live site doesn't show this design, so the full set of pages is on Behance.
